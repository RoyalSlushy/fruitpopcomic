# Aesthetic references

Binding visual direction for Fruit Pop Comic, pinned by the creator.

Per Impeccable's flow this is **not** `DESIGN.md`. The design system gets written
at finish, from the built world, by the documenter. This file records the pinned
reference material and the vocabulary for it so the direction survives a fresh
checkout.

## Source board

One reference board supplied by the creator, seven panels. The image file itself
is not yet in the repo — see [`references/`](references/) for where it goes.

## What the board actually shares

The seven look scattered by subject but converge hard on form:

- **Blue is the home ground.** Four of seven sit on saturated blue or navy; yellow
  is the counterweight. This is not a neutral-plus-accent world.
- **Chunky, tactile, outlined.** Thick borders, hard offset shadows, beveled and
  inflated buttons, pills and lozenges. Everything looks pressable and physical.
- **The tile is the layout atom.** Modular cards and grids carry structure, not
  columns of text.
- **Sticker logic.** Elements rotate off-axis, overlap, and layer as if adhered.
  Nothing is timidly aligned to a single grid.
- **Chrome as ornament.** Counters, badges, currency pills, timers, tab bars.
  Density and instrumentation read as richness, not clutter.
- **Mixed-script typography.** Latin next to CJK, tight tracked caps, condensed
  grotesques, speech-bubble lettering. Type as graphic object.
- **Stars, bursts, sparkles** as recurring punctuation.
- **Screen-native.** These are interfaces, not posters. The world's grammar is
  navigation, state, and HUD — that is what to build in.

## Vocabulary

The creator named *bold, vivid, stylish, y2k* and asked for the words they could
not place. The board is more specific than y2k:

**Closest named traditions**
- **Console-menu maximalism** — GameCube/DS-era first-party game UI. The single
  most accurate label for panels 1–2.
- **Gacha HUD** — contemporary mobile-game home screens: dense instrumentation
  layered over full-bleed character art. Panels 3–5.
- **Superflat** (Murakami lineage) — flat high-chroma color, pop iconography,
  depth by layering rather than rendering.
- **Decora / kirakira** — Harajuku sticker-and-sparkle accumulation. Panel 7.
- **Techo (手帳) collage** — Japanese planner/journaling culture: washi tape,
  photocards, foil, ring binding. Panel 7.
- **Retro-OS / GUI nostalgia** — Win98 chrome and Game Boy palettes as ornament.
  Panel 6.
- **Utility-tactical streetwear** — the tag chips, stencil caps, and hazard
  markings in panel 3.

**Useful adjectives**
high-chroma · chunky · beveled · inflated · jelly · glossy · toy-plastic ·
sticker-layered · off-axis · HUD-dense · instrumented · candy · bubblegum ·
checkerboard · maximalist · tactile · pressable · loud

**What it is not**
Not Frutiger Aero (no glass, no water, no lens flare). Not vaporwave (no
melancholy, no chrome gradients on black). Not flat-minimal, not editorial, not
"clean." Restraint is off the table.

## Craft consequence to carry forward

The one real tension worth naming, and it is a consequence, not a redirect: the
primary visitor is a cold reader arriving to **read a comic**. A HUD-dense,
sticker-layered world is exactly right for the shell — navigation, wiki chrome,
chapter tiles, badges, the hub — and it is exactly wrong pressed onto the comic
page itself, which needs to breathe and must not compete with its own frame.

So the maximalism lives in the chrome and the chrome quiets down around the art.
Every reference on this board already does this: panel 5 puts dense chrome around
full-bleed character art without covering the face. That is the move.
